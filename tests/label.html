Blind labelling

Both Free Bird evaluation sets were labelled by the project author, which is a real methodological weakness: it shows the system behaves as its designer intends, not that the designer reads text the way anyone else does. This tool closes that gap. You will see each case with no label and no prediction, and at the end it reports how often you agreed with the stored label, corrected for chance.

Before you start

There are no right answers here and you are not being tested. Pick the one thing that seems to be most weighing on the person. If two fit, pick the one you would mention first if you were describing the message to a friend.

It takes about ten minutes. Your answers stay in this browser under the key freebird.labelling and are never sent anywhere.

How agreement is scored

Raw agreement is misleading on its own, because two people picking at random from ten options would still agree sometimes. Cohen's kappa corrects for that: it measures agreement above what chance alone would produce. 1.0 is perfect, 0 is chance, and below 0 is worse than chance. The convention most people use is that 0.21 to 0.40 is fair, 0.41 to 0.60 is moderate, 0.61 to 0.80 is substantial, and above 0.80 is almost perfect.

A moderate kappa on a ten-class task about ambiguous emotional text is a normal and reportable result, not a failure. If it comes out low, the useful conclusion is that the label set itself is ambiguous, which is worth knowing and worth saying.
